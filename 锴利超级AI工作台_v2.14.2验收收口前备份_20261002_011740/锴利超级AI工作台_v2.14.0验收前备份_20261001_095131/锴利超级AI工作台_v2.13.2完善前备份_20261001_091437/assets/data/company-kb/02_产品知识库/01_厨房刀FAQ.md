**Q: What steel grades do you offer for kitchen knives?**
**A:** We cover the full range from entry to premium: entry line 3Cr13 (55–57 HRC) and 4Cr13 (56–58 HRC); our workhorse Yangjiang steel is 5Cr15MoV (57–59 HRC); mid-to-high upgrade options are 8Cr13MoV (59–61 HRC) and 9Cr18MoV (60–62 HRC); premium Damascus series uses VG10 core steel (65–67 HRC) with 67/110/129 layers. We also offer German 1.4116 and Outokumpu 1.4028/Dura 420. Our complete steel library covers 220 grades — ask us for the full spec sheet.

**Q: What is the difference between 5Cr15MoV and 9Cr18MoV?**
**A:** 5Cr15MoV is our volume-production workhorse martensitic stainless steel at 57–59 HRC — excellent balance of sharpness, corrosion resistance, and affordability, ideal for everyday chef knives and knife sets. 9Cr18MoV is a higher-carbon upgrade at 60–62 HRC, offering noticeably better edge retention for premium single-knife lines. Both are Yangjiang-made and fully tested under TÜV SÜD protocols.

**Q: What does "VG10 Damascus" mean? How many layers?**
**A:** Damascus in our lineup is pattern-welded steel with a VG10 core (VG10 Core). VG10 is a Japanese-clad core steel hardened to 65–67 HRC, surrounded by layered stainless steel that creates the distinctive wave/feather pattern. We offer 67-layer, 110-layer, and 129-layer Damascus. Always quote both the layer count and the core steel (VG10) together in listings and inquiries.

**Q: What is the difference between a Chinese Cleaver and a regular kitchen knife?**
**A:** A Chinese Cleaver (Chinese Cleaver — do not call it "Bone Knife") has a blade thickness of ≥4.0 mm and is designed for chopping through bones. A regular slicing chef knife is thinner and intended for slicing and dicing. We offer both Damascus cleavers and hand-forged high-carbon cleavers.

**Q: How do I care for and maintain my Damascus / high-carbon knife to prevent rust?**
**A:** Wipe the blade dry after each use; do not leave it wet or in the dishwasher. Damascus and high-carbon blades naturally develop a patina over time — this is normal and does not affect performance. Apply a light coat of food-safe mineral oil periodically, especially on high-carbon tools. Store in a knife block, sheath, or on a magnetic strip, not loose in a drawer.

**Q: What is Full Tang and why does it matter?**
**A:** Full Tang means the steel blade runs continuously through the handle from tip to butt, with handle scales riveted on both sides. It is the structural hallmark of a heavy-duty knife — offering superior strength, balance, and durability compared to partial tang or stamped construction. All our premium fixed-blade kitchen knives and outdoor hunting knives are Full Tang.

**Q: What is the hardness (HRC) of your knives? How is it tested?**

**Q: What is the difference between forged and stamped knives?**
**A:** Forged knives (Hand Forged / Yangjiang Hand Forging — an intangible cultural heritage craft) are shaped by hammering hot steel, resulting in a denser, stronger blade with better balance. Stamped knives are cut from a steel sheet — lighter and cheaper but generally less robust. Our HC and HM series are hand-forged; the SS stainless chef line is precision-made. We also offer Vacuum Heat Treatment (VHT) and Cryogenic Treatment for premium lines.
